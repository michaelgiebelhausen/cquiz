# Product Vision — cquiz

## 1. Vision & Mission

Make conversational understanding assessable across a whole class. Preserve what a student contributed, how peers perceived it, and why the system ranked them as it did. The founder prioritizes ranking but wants raw points preserved to inspect differences and score distributions.
Values: evidence before inference; no lost answers; one speaking turn at a time; private assessment; clear separation of mastery and presentation diagnostics.

## 2. User Research

The instructor needs reliable setup, visible readiness and table progress, intervention controls, and exports. Students need an uncomplicated phone screen that never reveals answer rubrics or standings. The existing alternative is a conventional test or sequential oral exam. Assumptions requiring classroom validation include the relationship between willingness and knowledge, speech recognition under room noise, and the reliability of peer competence judgments.
The journey: prepare content externally, validate imports, rehearse device setup, start the exam, monitor tables, supervise two mingles, finish, resolve failed evaluations, export evidence.

## 3. Product Strategy

The minimum complete product includes multi-table persistence, live text contribution collection, AI evaluation, required peer ratings, retrospective peer weights, three-round adaptive grouping, and administrator exports. Exclude content generation, conferencing, public ranking, official grade conversion, and appearance or fluency effects on assessment.
Success is a recoverable end-to-end exam. Automated evaluation is never silently replaced by rehearsal heuristics in a live exam. Current rank is a provisional estimate; evidence coverage and instability are visible to the instructor. Grouping is a documented heuristic, not a claim of validated item-response theory.

## 4. Brand Strategy

Student copy invites a contribution, not a performance: “Give your top insider tip.” Instructor copy explains state and recovery. Approved visual direction lives in docs/design.md. Both surfaces are tools in a bright classroom; branding should support attention rather than distract from peers.
